The short version

  • LP has no servers, no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Nobody — including the people who make LP — receives your notes, boards, calendar or anything about how you use it.
  • Your notes and boards are files in a folder you choose, on your Mac. LP reads and writes them there and nowhere else.
  • LP only goes online when you ask it to: to sync the calendar accounts you connect (Google, Microsoft) and to publish a board's progress page when you share one — to your own Google Drive or GitHub. Apple Calendar never leaves your Mac through LP.
  • LP takes only what it needs for each of those, keeps it on your Mac, and lets you remove it.

Who this is about

LP is free, open-source software (MIT License) that runs entirely on your Mac. It is not a service: there is no "LP company" or server that collects or stores data. When you connect Google, Microsoft or GitHub, LP talks to them directly from your Mac, with credentials that belong to you. Those companies handle the data they hold under their own privacy policies: Google · Microsoft · GitHub · Apple.

Connected calendar accounts

You can connect Apple Calendar, Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook. In plain words:

  • Apple Calendar — LP asks macOS for access to your calendars and reads and writes them on your Mac, including Google, Exchange and other accounts you add in System Settings → Internet Accounts. macOS does the syncing; LP itself sends nothing.
  • Google and Microsoft — you click Connect and sign in on Google's or Microsoft's own page, in a window LP asks macOS to keep private. For Google, LP signs in through its own app registration (a public "client ID", no secret) — or yours, if you set one under Advanced; for Microsoft, through your own registration for now (LP's own comes in a later version). LP then reads your calendars and their events, and changes events you edit in LP, by calling Google's or Microsoft's API directly from your Mac. There is no server in between: your sign-in stays in your Mac's Keychain, and the makers of LP never see it or your data.
  • LP asks for the smallest permissions that work: Google — see your list of calendars and view and edit events (not the permission to change calendars or who they are shared with); Microsoft — calendars and stay signed in (not your profile). Microsoft's calendar permission is broader than LP needs — it would also allow changing calendars and their sharing — because Microsoft has nothing narrower that lets an app edit events; LP only reads and edits events.
  • Disconnecting deletes the tokens from your Keychain and LP's copy of that account's events; for Google, LP also asks Google to end its access.

Details, risks and how to remove access completely: docs/CONNECTED_ACCOUNTS.md.

Sharing a board

Sharing is off until you turn it on for a board. When you click Share Progress, LP publishes the board's progress page through your own account — never through one of LP's:

  • In your Google Drive, as a Google Doc — through the Google account you connected for the calendar, once you allow it (Settings → Sharing → Allow Google Drive…). LP asks Google only for the files it creates (drive.file): it can't see or change anything else in your Drive.
  • On GitHub, as a secret gist — with a GitHub token you created that can only make gists.

Either way it's free, and:

  • The page contains the board's title, lists, card titles, due dates, tags, progress and recent activity. Card notes are never included.
  • Anyone who has the link can open it — no account needed. It isn't listed or searchable, but it isn't password-protected: a link that is passed on works for whoever gets it.
  • A Google Doc can only be read by others, not edited. LP doesn't put your name or email address on the page, but Google may show the people who open it which Google account owns the Doc — its name, and possibly its email address. If that matters, share from a separate Google account. Its earlier versions stay in the Doc's version history, which only you can see. Stop Sharing deletes the Doc, and disconnecting the Google account in Settings → Calendars first deletes the Docs this Mac made or keeps up to date in that Drive.
  • A gist shows your GitHub username, and GitHub keeps earlier versions of it in its history until you click Stop Sharing, which deletes the page and its history. Someone signed in to GitHub can comment on it or fork it. A separate free GitHub account just for sharing keeps your main username off the page.
  • Anyone who opened a page can keep a copy; Stop Sharing can't remove those.
  • LP doesn't track who opens the page. Google or GitHub hosts it and sees visits like any website (see Cookies).

To share without any online service, use Export Report…: it saves a self-contained HTML file you can send yourself.

Only the data that's needed

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, advertising or third-party code in LP.
  • Calendar: your calendars and their events from a year back to two years ahead (so they're there offline), plus months you open. LP asks Google and Microsoft for only what it shows (title, times, place, notes, link); when it creates an event in Outlook, Microsoft sends the new event back in full, and LP keeps only those fields. Attendees and organisers are never stored.
  • Sharing: card notes are left out; nothing is published until you choose to; in Google Drive LP can reach only the files it created.
  • Reminders and widgets use what is already on your Mac.
  • Logs: LP writes no logs containing your data.

Cookies

LP uses no cookies or tracking of any kind. See the Cookie Policy.

Security

LP is sandboxed by macOS and can only reach the folder you chose and files you pick. Tokens are in the Keychain; connections use HTTPS; sign-in uses PKCE with no secrets in the app; nothing in a note can make LP read or write outside your workspace or run code. See SECURITY.md.

Your choices

  • Disconnect an account in Settings → Calendars (the Docs this Mac shared from a Google account's Drive are deleted first); Stop Sharing a board in its progress panel; Disconnect GitHub in Settings → Sharing.
  • Remove LP's access at the source: Google · Microsoft personal accounts · Microsoft work or school · GitHub tokens.
  • Remove everything LP keeps: quit LP, delete the app, delete ~/Library/Containers/LTF.Local-Productivity/ (and, if LP was signed with your team — which keeps widget data there automatically — ~/Library/Group Containers/<team ID>.LTF.Local-Productivity/), delete the .lp folder in your workspace, and delete LP's items in Keychain Access (search "Local-Productivity"). Your notes stay as they are.

Google API data

LP's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from Google is used only to show and edit your calendar in LP on your Mac, and to publish the progress pages you choose to share to your own Drive; it is never transferred to anyone else, used for advertising, or read by people.

Children

LP is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone.
