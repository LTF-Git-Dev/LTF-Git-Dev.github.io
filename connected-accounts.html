LP keeps your notes on your Mac. It only talks to other services you connect yourself. This page explains, for each one, what LP can do, what it takes, where that goes, and how to remove access. The short version is in the Privacy Policy.

LP has no server. Every request goes from your Mac straight to Apple (through macOS), Google, Microsoft or GitHub over HTTPS — nobody sits in between, and the makers of LP never see anything.

How LP connects

Google and Microsoft only let an app sign in if it is registered with them. LP is registered with Google once, by its makers, so you just click Connect Google Account… and approve on Google's own page (LP's own Microsoft registration comes in a later version — see below):

  • Your sign-in goes straight from your Mac to Google or Microsoft and stays in your Keychain. LP has no server, so the makers of LP never see your sign-in or your data; the registration only tells Google and Microsoft which app is asking. Nothing goes through an "LP account" — there isn't one.
  • A registration's ID is public, like every desktop app's — not a password. LP uses no client secret, and sign-in uses PKCE, so nobody can take over a sign-in LP started. Someone could build a fake app that shows "LP" on the sign-in page, as with any app: get LP only from its official download.
  • Google reviews apps that ask for calendar access before they can sign people in without an "unverified app" warning.
  • Microsoft: LP's own registration comes in a later version — for now, connect Outlook with your own registration (Advanced) or through your Mac (below). Work or school accounts may need their organisation's approval.
  • No registration at all: add the account in System Settings → Internet Accounts and connect Apple Calendar in LP — macOS signs in and syncs. (Microsoft 365 work accounts may not sync on a Mac until Apple updates macOS for a change Microsoft is making; Drive sharing needs a direct Google connection.)
  • Your own registration (Advanced): Settings → Calendars → Use Your Own Client ID, with the steps. For Google, a Personal mode project: only your own account can use it, with no review, no warning and no 7-day limit. A connection keeps the registration it was made with (its tokens remember it), so changing the setting only affects new sign-ins — but board pages shared to Google Drive can only be reached through the registration that made them: stop sharing them before you switch.

Apple Calendar

  • Access: macOS's calendar permission ("full access"), asked for only when you connect.
  • What LP does: reads events around the months you look at and the weeks around today (for reminders and widgets), and creates, changes or deletes events when you do so in LP.
  • Stored: not in LP's calendar cache — macOS keeps them and syncs them with iCloud or other accounts you set up in System Settings. If you use LP's widgets, the next few events (from every calendar) are in the widgets' data so they can be shown.
  • Remove: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars → turn off LP.

Google Calendar

  • Permissions LP asks for (and nothing else):
    • calendar.calendarlist.readonly — see the list of calendars you have;
    • calendar.events — view and edit events on your calendars.

    LP doesn't ask for the full calendar permission, which could also change or delete calendars and who they are shared with. Only if you allow Google Drive for sharing (Settings → Sharing), one more: drive.file — see, edit and delete only the files LP creates in your Drive.

  • What LP fetches: your calendar list (name, colour, whether you can edit it) and the events from a year back to two years ahead (plus any other months you open), limited to title, times, place, notes, a link to the event, and its version. Attendees, organisers, attachments and the like are not requested.
  • Where it goes: a cache in LP's container (~/Library/Containers/LTF.Local-Productivity/Data/Library/Application Support/LP/calendar-cache.json), so the calendar shows at once; the refresh and access tokens in your Keychain.
  • Disconnect (Settings → Calendars): first deletes the board pages LP shared in that account's Drive (if some can't be — offline, say — it asks before going on), then deletes the tokens and that account's events from LP — even if its client ID was cleared — and asks Google to end LP's access — for that account on your other Macs too, which then need connecting again (they use the same registration). A sign-in right after waits for that to finish. To be sure, also check Google Account → Third-party connections.
  • Good to know (your own client ID only): an External Google project in Testing ends sign-ins after 7 days, and publishing one needs a website; a Personal mode project has neither problem.

Microsoft Outlook

  • Permissions LP asks for: Calendars.ReadWrite and offline_access (stay connected). LP doesn't ask to read your profile; it learns which account is connected from your default calendar's owner. Calendars.ReadWrite is broader than LP needs — it would also allow changing calendars and their sharing — but Microsoft has no narrower permission that lets an app edit events. LP only reads and edits events.
  • What LP fetches: your calendars and their events from a year back to two years ahead (plus any other months you open), limited to subject, notes, place, times, whether all-day, series, link and version. When LP creates an event, Microsoft sends the new event back in full; LP keeps only those fields.
  • Where it goes: the same cache and Keychain as Google.
  • Disconnect: deletes the tokens and that account's events from LP. Microsoft has no way for an app to end its own access, so remove it yourself at Microsoft account → Apps and services (personal) or My Apps (work or school).

Google Drive (sharing a board)

  • Allowing it: Settings → Sharing → Allow Google Drive… next to a Google account you connected for the calendar. Google's page asks for one more permission, drive.file — only the files LP creates; LP can't see or change anything else in your Drive. In the Google Cloud project of your own client ID (if you use one), turn on the Google Drive API first.
  • What LP sends: for each board you share there, its progress page as a Google Doc named "<board> — Progress" (title, lists, card titles, due dates, tags, progress, recent activity — never card notes), updated a few seconds after the board changes if you leave automatic updates on. The link stays the same.
  • Who can see it: anyone with the link can read it — not edit it — without signing in; it isn't searchable. LP doesn't put your name or email address on the page, but Google may show the people who open it which Google account owns the Doc (its name, possibly its email address) — share from a separate Google account if that matters. Earlier versions stay in the Doc's version history, which only you can see.
  • What LP keeps: which Docs this Mac made — or keeps up to date ("Keep It Updated From Here") — by account, in LP's preferences, so it can delete them. A Doc is on that list from the moment it exists, before anyone else can see it.
  • Stop Sharing deletes the Doc (its link stops working within seconds). Disconnecting the account in Settings → Calendars deletes those Docs from its Drive first — after waiting for any page still being shared, while no new one starts; if some can't be deleted, LP asks before disconnecting anyway and says they are still in that Drive.
  • Cost: free; each page takes a few kilobytes of your Drive storage.

GitHub (sharing a board)

  • Token: a classic token with only the gist permission, which you create on GitHub and paste into Settings → Sharing. It can make gists — nothing else on your account.
  • What LP sends: for each board you share, its progress page (title, lists, card titles, due dates, tags, progress, recent activity — never card notes). It is updated a few seconds after the board changes, if you leave automatic updates on.
  • Who can see it: anyone with the link; it isn't listed or searchable. The page shows your GitHub username, and GitHub keeps earlier versions in the page's history until you stop sharing. Anyone who opened it can keep a copy, and someone signed in to GitHub can comment on it or fork it; Stop Sharing can't remove those. To keep your main username off it, connect a separate free GitHub account just for sharing.
  • Other people's sharing uses their own GitHub accounts. Your token is kept in your Keychain and sent only to GitHub, over HTTPS, to make and update your gists — never to viewers or anyone else.
  • Stop Sharing deletes the page and its history. Disconnect in Settings → Sharing removes the token from LP; delete the token on GitHub too if you no longer need it.
  • Cost: free — gists are part of every GitHub account, including free ones.

Without internet

  • Google and Outlook: while LP is running and online it syncs them in full — a year back to two years ahead, about every half hour (also when LP is behind other apps; not while the Mac sleeps), and right away with the calendar's Sync button — and keeps that copy on your Mac until you disconnect. So the calendar, reminders and widgets work offline. Changes need the internet: offline, LP says it couldn't make them and puts the event back as it was — they aren't saved for later.
  • Apple Calendar works fully offline, changes included — macOS keeps it and syncs later. For offline editing of Google or Outlook events, add the account in System Settings → Internet Accounts and connect Apple Calendar in LP instead of connecting it directly.

When a service asks LP to slow down

Google, Microsoft and GitHub limit how often an app may call them. If one of them answers "too many requests", LP waits as long as it asks — at most an hour — before calling it again, and says so:

  • Calendar: Settings → Calendars shows "The calendar service asked LP to slow down. Please wait until …" next to the account. Nothing is sent to that account until then: a sync under way stops, and a change you try meanwhile is turned down at once (LP says so and puts the event back). LP syncs the account again by itself afterwards.
  • Sharing: the progress panel shows "GitHub asked LP to slow down. Please wait until …". Nothing is sent to GitHub with that token until then, for any board (another token, for another account, isn't held up). Automatic updates try again a few times, each waiting longer; after that your next change, or Update Now, brings the page up to date.

For calendars you don't need to do anything. A shared page catches up by itself too, unless the service keeps asking LP to wait: then the progress panel says automatic updates are paused, and your next change or Update Now brings the page up to date. Normally none of this happens: LP syncs about every 5 minutes while it is in front (a full sync at most every half hour) and updates a shared page once, 4 seconds after the last change.

The risks, plainly

  • A connected account works on this Mac. Anyone who can use your unlocked Mac account can use LP and see your calendar. Lock your Mac and keep FileVault on.
  • Tokens last until revoked. They are encrypted in the Keychain, but if your Mac account is compromised they could be used. Disconnecting removes them; revoking at the source (links above) makes sure.
  • Events in the cache are protected by your Mac login and FileVault, like your other files.
  • Reminders and widgets show event and task titles on screen, including notification banners; choose in System Settings → Notifications whether they appear on the lock screen.
  • A shared link can be passed on. Treat a shared board like anything else you publish.
  • Sign-in privacy depends on your default browser. LP asks for a private sign-in session; Safari honours it, other browsers may not.
  • Widget data: when LP is signed with your team, it keeps today's tasks and the next events (title, time, place) in ~/Library/Group Containers/<team ID>.LTF.Local-Productivity/ for the widgets — whether or not you have added a widget. Disconnecting an account removes its events there too.

For developers

The permissions live in GoogleCalendarMapping.configuration (the Drive permission only with includeDrive, GoogleCalendarMapping.driveFileScope) and MicrosoftGraphMapping.configuration, the fetched fields in GoogleCalendarMapping.eventListFields and MicrosoftGraphMapping.eventFields, and Drive's requests in GoogleDriveMapping; the calendar and board tests check that they stay this narrow. Ask for a broader permission only when a feature truly needs it, and update this page and the privacy policy in the same change.
